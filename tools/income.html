Four different "tax rates"

Which one someone means decides whether the same paycheck is "30% tax" or "40% tax". The marginal rate is measured by actually re-running the whole calculation with $1,000 more wages, so it captures bracket changes, the Social Security cap, phase-outs and credits.

Sources

Federal brackets, standard deductions, capital gains thresholds, child tax credit: IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, as tabulated by the Tax Foundation.
California 2026 rate schedules, standard deduction and exemption credits: FTB Tax News, October 2026. The exemption-credit phase-out thresholds are 2025 figures pending the FTB's December release.
Retirement and health account limits: IRS Notice 2025-67 and Rev. Proc. 2025-19, as summarized by Mercer Advisors.
Social Security wage base and payroll rates: Social Security Administration; California SDI rate: EDD.
SALT cap, senior deduction, charitable floor and non-itemizer charitable deduction: Public Law 119-21 (the 2025 reconciliation act) as adjusted for 2026.

What this page does not model

Alternative minimum tax (matters mainly for ISO exercises and very large itemized deductions). Capital loss netting and the $3,000 loss limit. The itemized-deduction haircut in the 37% bracket. The $6,000 senior deduction. Earned income credit and education credits. Self-employment income. Local taxes (California has no city income tax). Payroll withholding timing: employers withhold bonus and RSU income at flat supplemental rates (22% federal up to $1 million, 10.23% California), so a paystub can show a higher rate than the true annual liability computed here, with the difference returned as a refund or collected as a balance due in April.

Terms used on this page

Gross pay
Everything your employer pays you in wages before anything is taken out: base, cash bonus, the market value of RSUs on the day they vest, and the bargain element of ESPP shares or option exercises.
RSU vesting
When restricted stock units vest, the share value that day is added to your W-2 as ordinary wages, whether you sell or hold. Your employer usually sells or withholds some shares to cover withholding. If you later sell, only the change in price since vesting is a capital gain or loss.
Pre-tax deduction
Money taken from pay before income tax is computed. Traditional 401(k) lowers income tax but not Social Security, Medicare or SDI. Cafeteria-plan items (health premiums, HSA, FSAs, commuter) lower every tax. California does not recognize HSA deductions.
After-tax deduction
Money taken from pay after tax: Roth 401(k), ESPP purchases, after-tax 401(k). They reduce the deposit but not the tax.
Adjusted gross income (AGI)
Taxable wages plus investment income and other income, before the standard or itemized deduction. Phase-outs for credits and the SALT cap are measured against it.
Standard deduction
A fixed amount subtracted from AGI before the brackets apply. Federal: $16,100 single, $32,200 joint, $24,150 head of household. California: $5,900 single, $11,800 joint or head of household. It is a deduction, not a tax.
Itemized deductions
Mortgage interest, state and local taxes (federal cap $40,400 in 2026, shrinking above $505,000 AGI), and charitable gifts (above a 0.5% of AGI floor). Used instead of the standard deduction when larger. California allows property tax and mortgage interest but never state income tax.
Taxable income
AGI minus the deduction. The number the bracket rates are applied to.
Tax bracket / marginal rate
Only dollars inside a bracket pay its rate. Your marginal rate is the combined federal, state and payroll rate on the next dollar, which is what a raise, bonus or RSU vest is actually taxed at.
Qualified dividends and long-term gains
Taxed federally at 0%, 15% or 20% depending on total taxable income, stacked on top of ordinary income. California taxes them as ordinary income at up to 12.3%.
Net investment income tax (NIIT)
An extra 3.8% federal tax on investment income once AGI exceeds $200,000 single or $250,000 joint. The thresholds are not indexed for inflation.
Child tax credit
$2,200 per child under 17 and $500 per other dependent, reduced by $50 for every $1,000 of AGI above $200,000 single or $400,000 joint. A credit cuts the tax itself; a deduction cuts the income the tax is computed on.
FICA
Social Security (6.2% on wages up to $184,500 per worker) and Medicare (1.45% on all wages, plus 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax above $200,000 single or $250,000 joint).
SDI
California State Disability Insurance, 1.3% of all wages with no cap. It is deductible as a state tax on a federal itemized return.
Exemption credit
California subtracts $158 per filer and $491 per dependent directly from the tax. It phases out slowly for high incomes.
Effective rate
Total tax divided by income. Always below the marginal rate because the lower brackets took their smaller bite first. Dividing by taxable income instead of gross gives a larger number for the same tax.
20/4/10 rule
Car-buying guideline: 20% down, a loan no longer than 4 years, all car costs under 10% of gross income.
